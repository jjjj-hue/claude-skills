# Week 1 (W28): Jul 12, 2026 — The Approval Loop

Lucia turns Claude Code inward on itself for an afternoon, then spends the evening trapped in a single blast campaign that won't leave the launchpad.

## Narrative

The week opens with Lucia doing something she hasn't done before in this workspace: auditing the workspace itself. Session 1 starts as a memory search, but by S2 and S3 she's asking Claude to analyze the Bond NY automation setup and audit skills/plugins for overlap, poking at `npx skills find` before deciding to build custom tooling instead. It's a rare moment of the tool looking in the mirror — and it keeps looking. By mid-afternoon a plugin usage audit surfaces heavy reliance on hookify, remember, and superpowers (obs 17), followed by a full configuration audit (obs 19). Then, in the week's clearest bit of meta-recursion, Lucia runs `/session-report` and gets back a report about her own reporting habits: email-blast and web scraping dominate skill usage, the top prompt accounts for 3.63% of activity, and the tool that measures the work confirms the work is mostly building emails (obs 21–34). A session-analyzer analyzing sessions, finding that the sessions are mostly about analyzing listings.

Alongside the audit, new infrastructure gets stood up. Composio CLI gets authenticated and mapped to a Follow Up Boss toolkit (obs 2–5), which becomes the seed for an FUB Lead Score Playground — deployed to Claude artifacts within the hour (obs 6–7). Google Sheets sync for the qualified-bank.md tracker follows (obs 32–34), giving Lucia a live spreadsheet mirror of her qualified leads. A disk-space investigation (11GB in Application Support) and an HTML blast template cleanup round out the day's tooling work — blast tables get normalized to a 6-column schema (obs 27), and outputs get consolidated into one directory per new memory rules.

Then, at 3:58 PM, the real story of the week begins: the 232 Elizabeth Street flip. What starts as "finish 2-bed flip alternatives" (S31) becomes an hours-long, session-churning ordeal. Claude scrapes Greystone listings, batch-pulls 8 unit detail pages via Firecrawl, and starts building a unit tracker — published as an interactive artifact (obs 52, 55). But data quality keeps intruding: 117 West 13th #64 turns out already rented, 222 East 39th doesn't exist on StreetEasy, 201 West 11th has a price discrepancy (obs 47–50). Each fix triggers a fresh session, and from roughly 6:48 PM to 7:34 PM the log is nearly wall-to-wall identical session titles — "232 Elizabeth Flip blast campaign — awaiting user approval to send" — climbing from 13 to 16 to 17 to 19 to 20 of 23 units ready, one promotion at a time (Atlas #30D, Atlas #46A, Aire #25H flip-flopping between Ready and Needs Info, The Maybury renamed from "Marbury"). A stop-hook mechanism is explicitly named as blocking progress (S109). Eventually the campaign fragments into "volumes" — Volume 1 settles at 9 units, $5,100–$8,550, gets HTML-ified and saved as "FUB Email – 232 Elizabeth Flip Vol4" (obs 75) — before the day closes with a late-night pass (10:57–10:59 PM) adding 3D tour links and address clarifications for Mercedes House and 70 Battery Place.

The week ends on S249: Lucia invokes `/review` but the session needs scope clarification — a small loose thread into whatever comes next.
